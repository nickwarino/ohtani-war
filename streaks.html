One club, every window length at once

A season's best 10 games and its best 100 games are different achievements, and a club can be historic at one length and unremarkable at another. Pick a team season and this walks every window from 10 games up, plotting where that run stands against every other club season on record.

How to read this. A "stretch" is any run of consecutive regular-season games, game 12 through 61 of a season, say. Playoff games are excluded throughout.

Within a season, or across. The switch at the top decides whether a stretch has to live inside one season or may carry from September into April. Within a season is the default because that is how records are normally kept, and because it compares like with like: every club season gets one entry. Across seasons, each franchise's whole run of games closes up into one continuous log, so the 1986 Brewers finishing hot and the 1987 Brewers starting hot become a single streak. Elo survives the crossing: the total is a sum of per-game changes, and the reversion toward the league average that happens between seasons is not a game, so it never enters the count. A cross-winter climb is Elo earned on the field.

Ties count as half a win in every winning percentage here, the way the standings have always treated them. Over a window of N games that works out to (N + W − L) / 2N, so 44–5–1 reads .890 rather than .898. Ties effectively vanished after 1970; before that they are common enough to move the order.

The loss budget. Setting it to zero gives plain winning streaks. Setting it to three asks how many wins a club piled up before its fourth loss, over the best such run it ever had. Each run is trimmed to start and end on a win, so the count is not padded by ties on the ends, and each club gets its single best run rather than every window that fits the budget.

Overlapping runs. A hot 50-game stretch contains dozens of other very good 50-game stretches that share most of the same games. By default the lists hide those near-duplicates and show a club's streak once. Flip the switch to see every stretch, which is what the scatterplot's full cloud is made of.

Rank, in the last chart. A club season's rank at a given window length counts every other club season once: 7th at 28 games means six other club seasons had a better 28-game stretch somewhere in their year, not six near-copies of one hot run. The overlap switch above does not change it. Small windows tie heavily (plenty of clubs have gone 10-0), so the headline picks the longest window at which the club ranks highest, which is the harder achievement.

Elo is FiveThirtyEight's rating: every club starts around 1500, wins and losses move the number by an amount that depends on the opponent's strength, the margin, and home field. Roughly 25 points of Elo is worth one win over a full season.
